## Notes

The client runs a small operations team that manually triages inbound support emails, updates spreadsheets, and assigns follow-up tasks in Slack. They want to reduce repetitive work but are worried about mistakes, customer tone, and staff adoption.

Goals:

- Reduce manual triage time.
- Keep humans in control of customer-facing responses.
- Create better visibility into repeated support themes.
- Avoid a risky all-at-once automation rollout.

Constraints:

- Customer data is sensitive.
- The team uses Gmail, Google Sheets, and Slack.
- There is no internal engineering team available for ongoing maintenance.
- The first pilot should show value within four weeks.

Risks:

- Automation could send an incorrect or off-brand response.
- Staff may ignore the tool if it creates extra review work.
- Integrations may be fragile without clear ownership.

Open questions:

- Which support categories are safe to auto-draft?
- Who approves suggested replies?
- What data can be stored outside existing systems?
- What metric defines a successful pilot?

Next actions:

- Map the current support triage workflow.
- Collect twenty anonymized support examples.
- Define safe automation boundaries.
- Propose a four-week pilot with human approval gates.
