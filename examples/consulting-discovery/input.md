### Turn 2 — Assistant

For a one-month income window, the best path is usually **service-first SaaS** rather than pure SaaS.

The goal is to sell a clear outcome manually or semi-manually first, then productize the repeated parts later.

A useful framing:

> Start by selling implementation or concierge delivery. Build software only around what repeats.

## Strongest starting options

### Option 1 — Internal knowledge assistant

This is strategically aligned with document ingestion, retrieval, knowledge organization, and reusable context.

Example offer:

> I will build a private assistant over your existing company knowledge base in 7–10 days, with source references and simple admin controls.

Potential buyers:

- small consultancies
- agencies
- startups
- operations-heavy teams
- support-heavy businesses

Why it fits:

- recurring need
- strong technical credibility
- reusable implementation patterns
- can later become a productized service or SaaS

### Option 2 — AI workflow automation sprint

This is likely the fastest path to near-term revenue because it sells an implementation outcome, not a platform.

Example workflows:

- lead form → classify request → draft response → update CRM
- meeting transcript → action items → task tracker
- support ticket → classify → suggested reply
- customer form → quote or proposal draft
- spreadsheet → report → email summary

Example offer:

> In one week, I will identify and automate repetitive workflows in your business using AI, APIs, and lightweight tools.

Why it fits:

- direct business value
- low need for a polished product
- can reuse templates across clients
- creates case studies quickly

### Option 3 — Lead research and outreach assistant

This is commercially attractive because buyers connect it to pipeline and revenue.

Example offer:

> I will deliver a weekly list of qualified leads with research notes and personalized outreach drafts.

Risks:

- crowded space
- quality expectations can be high
- must avoid spammy positioning
- needs narrow targeting to work

## Suggested 30-day validation path

| Week | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Pick a niche and package the offer | Landing page, demo videos, prospect list |
| Week 2 | Run small pilot outreach | Discovery calls, discounted pilots |
| Week 3 | Deliver manually with scripts and AI | Working client results, before/after documentation |
| Week 4 | Productize repeated pieces | Templates, reusable code, case study, refined offer |

## Recommendation

Use the **AI workflow automation sprint** as the near-term income vehicle.

Use the repeated implementation patterns to inform longer-term products such as internal knowledge assistants, document workflow tools, or context-management systems.

### Turn 8 — Assistant

For the B2B AI workflow automation sprint, use a **service-led GTM**, not an ads-first or SaaS-first GTM.

The core offer:

> In 7–10 days, I will identify and automate 2–3 repetitive workflows in your business using AI, APIs, and lightweight tools, with documentation and handoff.

## Positioning

Do not lead with:

> I build AI automations.

That is too generic.

Better positioning options:

| Angle | Message |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Save 5–15 hours/week by automating admin, reporting, email, and document workflows. |
| Revenue | Turn messy leads, emails, notes, and client requests into faster follow-ups. |
| Operations | Reduce manual handoffs between forms, spreadsheets, CRMs, email, and internal tools. |
| AI adoption | Move from occasional AI use to practical workflows inside the business. |

Best early positioning:

> AI workflow automation for small teams already doing repetitive work in email, spreadsheets, CRMs, forms, documents, or support inboxes.

## Initial audiences

Do not start with “all small businesses.” Start with one or two audiences where the pain is obvious.

| Audience | Why promising | Example workflows |
|---|---|---|
| Agencies and consultants | Repetitive client work; understand time value | proposals, onboarding, reporting, lead research |
| Recruiting or staffing firms | Email, resumes, candidate summaries | CV screening, candidate outreach, client summaries |
| Real estate or property businesses | Leads, listings, documents, follow-ups | inquiry routing, listing descriptions, document extraction |
| Small SaaS/startups | Faster decision-making; tool-friendly | support triage, onboarding, internal knowledge assistant |
| Accounting/bookkeeping firms | Repetitive document workflows | invoice extraction, client reminders, month-end summaries |
| Training/coaching businesses | Leads, content, students, follow-ups | student summaries, content repurposing, lead nurturing |

Recommended first audiences:

1. Agencies/consultants
2. Small SaaS/startups

## Metrics and validation

Track the funnel weekly.

| Stage | Metric | Good early signal |
|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | Targeted prospects added | consistent weekly list |
| Outreach | Positive reply rate | meaningful replies, not just likes |
| Discovery | Calls booked | calls from people with real workflows |
| Sales | Paid pilot conversion | some discovery calls convert to paid pilots |
| Delivery | Time to first useful workflow | under 7–10 days |
| Value | Hours saved or workflow speed | buyer can describe clear value |
| Expansion | Follow-on work | client asks about additional workflows |
| Referral | Testimonials/referrals | at least some after early clients |

## Offer/audience fit signals

You are on the right track if:

- prospects understand the offer quickly
- they can name a workflow they want automated
- calls focus on “how would this work?” rather than “why do we need this?”
- some discovery calls convert into pilots
- clients ask for more workflows after the first one
- delivery becomes partially reusable across clients

You are not on the right track if:

- people say “interesting” but cannot name a painful workflow
- calls become general AI education
- every project is completely different
- buyers want a platform but have no budget
- prospects like the idea but avoid paying for implementation

| Role | Best tasks | Payment model |
|---|---|---|
| Lead researcher | Build prospect lists and collect company notes | hourly or per qualified list |
| Appointment setter | Send approved messages and book qualified calls | base plus bonus per qualified call |
| Sales assistant | CRM, follow-ups, scheduling | hourly |
| Copywriter | landing page, outreach copy, case study | fixed project |
| Video editor | polish demo videos | per video package |
| Automation helper | simpler no-code flows and documentation | hourly or per workflow |

Keep these yourself initially:

- positioning
- discovery calls
- sales calls
- solution design
- final delivery quality
- case study narrative

## Best alternative: white-label and subcontracting partnerships

Instead of selling directly to small businesses, sell to people who already have client trust.

Potential partners:

- web design agencies
- no-code agencies
- CRM consultants
- marketing agencies
- bookkeeping or operations consultants
- fractional operations leaders
- business coaches

Why this helps:

- they already have trust
- they already have clients
- they may want to sell AI but lack delivery depth

Better operating model:

1. Founder defines ICP and offer.
2. Lead researcher builds a targeted list.
3. Appointment setter sends compliant email or phone outreach.
4. Founder takes only qualified calls.

|---|---|---|

| Appointment setter | Send outreach, follow up, book calls | base plus qualified-call bonus |
| Sales assistant | CRM, scheduling, follow-up tracking | hourly |

## Qualification criteria for booked calls

Only pay bonuses for meetings that meet clear criteria:

- decision-maker or partner at a relevant business
- has a repetitive workflow pain
- has a budget or client opportunity
- agrees to discuss a paid sprint or audit
- not merely curious about AI in general

Instead:

- let them manage the client relationship

### Week 3 — Add delegated outreach

- list building

Start with one narrow ICP.

### Week 4 — Evaluate and double down

Review:

- which channel produced qualified calls

- which offer required the least explanation

|---|---:|---|

## Metrics that matter

| Metric | Early interpretation |

## Strongest recommended order

1. White-label agency partnerships
2. Paid workflow-audit funnel
3. High-intent search ads
4. Niche newsletter/community sponsorships
5. Paid appointment setting after the ICP and script are clearer

# Extractable evaluation graph

## Primary goal

Find a realistic short-term income path for a solo technical founder using AI-assisted development and marketing.

## Constraints

- Income should be possible within roughly one month.

## Main decision

Prioritize a **B2B AI workflow automation sprint** for near-term cash flow, while keeping B2C personal archive/context products as longer-term product-learning options.

## Important assumptions

| Assumption | Why it matters | How to test |
|---|---|---|
| Businesses have repetitive workflows that AI can improve | Core demand assumption | workflow audits and discovery calls |

| Trade-off | Chosen direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| B2B vs B2C | B2B first for revenue | fewer customers needed; higher ticket |
| SaaS vs service | service-first | faster validation and cash flow |
| direct sales vs borrowed trust | borrowed trust emphasized | direct channels are constrained |
| broad ads vs high-intent search | high-intent search only | better signal and less waste |
| platform marketplaces vs subcontracting | subcontracting/white-label | avoids new-profile trust problem |

## Suggested next actions

1. Choose one narrow ICP, preferably agencies/consultants or small tool-friendly teams.
